## Context

An infrastructure owner responsible for a coastal reinforced concrete asset needed a clear maintenance decision within a
fixed budget window. Available data included recent condition surveys, cover depth measurements, and exposure
classification. New testing was not feasible within schedule.

## Objectives

- Quantify corrosion risk with uncertainty, not single-point estimates
- Compare do-nothing vs intervention scenarios over a 30-year horizon
- Produce an action ranking that the owner could defend internally

## Inputs used

- Asset basics (age, geometry, materials, service requirements)
- Exposure class (chlorides, moisture cycles, temperature)
- Inspection records (condition survey, cover, chloride indicators)
- Decision horizon and budget constraints

## What we delivered (DIA)

- **Corrosion risk profile** with dominant mechanisms and sensitivity drivers
- **Service-life envelopes** at P10 / P50 / P90
- **Life-cycle cost trade-offs** for timing-sensitive interventions
- **Prioritized actions** with trigger thresholds

## Outcome

The owner used the DIA to align internal stakeholders on a staged intervention plan and deferred non-critical scope until
future budget cycles. The deliverable served as a decision brief for both engineering and finance teams.

## Lessons learned

- Early alignment on exposure assumptions reduces revision cycles
- Even limited inspection data can produce decision-ready envelopes when uncertainty is explicit
- Portfolio context matters: a single asset decision improved scheduling for adjacent assets
